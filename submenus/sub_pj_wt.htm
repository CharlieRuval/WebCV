Wind turbine inspection
As part of an engineering project, I reviewed current practices for the visual inspection of wind turbine generators, with particular attention to the use of unmanned aerial vehicles in constrained or restricted environments. This review provided a clear understanding of the capabilities and limitations of existing approaches and emphasized the value of structured, repeatable inspection procedures.
I modeled the complete inspection workflow by defining the logical and physical steps involved in an unmanned aerial vehicle based inspection. To represent system behavior, I used finite state machines to describe operational phases such as initialization, navigation, image acquisition, and return to the launch point. This structure established a clear and traceable sequence of actions.
To improve organization and scalability, I proposed a modeling approach that separates high level planning from execution level behavior. This resulted in a hierarchical discrete event systems model in which multiple layers of finite state machines operate with well defined responsibilities. Each layer addressed a different level of abstraction, making the overall model easier to maintain and adapt.
I also applied supervisory control methods to manage transitions between states and to ensure adherence to defined logic and safety conditions. The supervisor functioned as a decision layer that enabled or restricted actions based on system status and predefined rules.
