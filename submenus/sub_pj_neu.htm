Laboratory infrastructure for automation training
I participated in the development of new infrastructure to enhance laboratory practices at ITESO, contributing to the improvement of hands-on learning experiences for students. This work included designing pneumatic components in SolidWorks, transforming conceptual ideas into precise 3D models ready for fabrication. I manufactured and assembled these elements by laser cutting acrylic bases and integrating them into functional prototypes. To ensure accuracy and reliability, I performed detailed measurements, created technical drafts, and calibrated new instruments for optimal operation within the laboratory setups. Additionally, I modified a pneumatic robot arm to enable control through a PLC, expanding its capabilities and providing students with a more authentic industrial training experience that connects academic practice with real-world automation systems.
Furthermore, as part of this initiative to strengthen ITESO’s laboratory infrastructure, an industrial design model titled Modelo industrial de base reconfigurable para el montaje deslizable de componentes industriales para la automatización industrial was registered before the Instituto Mexicano de la Propiedad Industrial (IMPI) under the application number MX/f/2024/003522, published in the November 2024 issue of the Gaceta de Diseño Industrial.
