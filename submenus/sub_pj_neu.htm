Laboratory infrastructure for automation training
I participated in the development of new laboratory infrastructure to strengthen automation training at ITESO, contributing to improved practical learning experiences for students. This work included the design of pneumatic components in SolidWorks, where I transformed conceptual ideas into precise three dimensional models suitable for fabrication. I also manufactured and assembled these elements by laser cutting acrylic bases and integrating them into functional prototypes. To ensure accuracy and reliability, I performed detailed measurements, prepared technical drawings, and calibrated instruments to support consistent operation within the laboratory setups.
In addition, I modified a pneumatic robot arm to enable control through a PLC, expanding its functionality and providing students with an industrially oriented training platform that connects academic practice with automation systems used in professional environments.
Furthermore, as part of this initiative to strengthen ITESO's laboratory infrastructure, an industrial design model titled Modelo industrial de base reconfigurable para el montaje deslizable de componentes industriales para la automatización industrial was registered before the Instituto Mexicano de la Propiedad Industrial under the application number MX/f/2024/003522, published in the November 2024 issue of the Gaceta de Diseño Industrial.
